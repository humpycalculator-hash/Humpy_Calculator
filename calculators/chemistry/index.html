About the Chemistry calculators

These tools cover four common jobs in chemistry homework and lab preparation: molar mass from a formula, molarity, pH and hydrogen-ion concentration, and the ideal gas law. Each result comes with the formula and the working.

Always check your teacher’s or lab manual’s rules for units and significant figures. These calculators are meant to help you learn the method and check your own work.

Tips for accurate results

  • Write molar-mass formulas in simple form, such as H2O, NaCl or C6H12O6. Capital letters matter: Co is cobalt, CO is carbon monoxide. Parentheses are not accepted.
  • Molarity needs the volume in litres. To convert millilitres, divide by 1000.
  • In the Ideal Gas Law you can enter temperature in kelvin or degrees Celsius, and choose the pressure and volume units you have.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a chemical formula?

Use element symbols with numbers after them, like H2O or C6H12O6, with no spaces. The first letter of a symbol is capital and the second, if there is one, is lowercase. Formulas with parentheses, such as Ca(OH)2, are not accepted, so write every atom out, for example CaO2H2.

What does the pH number mean?

pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]. At 25 °C, a pH below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral and above 7 is basic. Each whole pH step is a tenfold change in hydrogen-ion concentration.

Which gas constant does the Ideal Gas Law use?

R = 8.314462618 Pa·m³·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹. Your inputs are converted to pascals, cubic metres and kelvin before the calculation, and the answer is shown in practical units as well.

How much volume does one mole of gas take?

At 273.15 K and 1 atm, one mole of an ideal gas takes up about 22.4 litres. You can check this by choosing Volume as the unknown with n = 1, T = 273.15 K and P = 1 atm.

Where do the atomic masses come from?

They are standard atomic masses built into the calculator, and the molar mass is shown in g/mol with the steps for each element.
