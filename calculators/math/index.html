About the Math calculators

These tools cover the calculations students and everyday users need most: percentages, square roots, powers, logarithms, fractions, averages, shapes, linear equations and GPA. Every calculator shows the formula and the working step by step, so you can follow the method and not only copy an answer.

Everything runs in your browser. The numbers you type are not sent anywhere, which makes the tools handy for homework, grade checks and quick everyday sums.

Tips for accurate results

  • For the Percentage calculator, type the percent in the first box and the full value in the second. For example, 20 and 150 gives 20% of 150.
  • For the average, separate the numbers with commas, such as 4, 8, 15, 16, 23.
  • For GPA, pick the grading scale that matches your university first. You can edit the grade points, and your scale is remembered in your browser.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the GPA calculator with my university’s grading scale?

Yes. Choose one of the built-in 4.0 scales or select Custom and type your own grade points. The scale you set is saved in your own browser only, so it is still there next time you visit on the same device.

What is the difference between GPA, SGPA and CGPA?

GPA is the average grade point across the courses you enter, weighted by credit hours. SGPA is the same calculation for a single semester. CGPA combines all of your semesters, again weighted by credit hours.

How do I find a percentage of a number?

Enter the percentage and the number. The calculator multiplies the number by the percentage divided by 100. For 20% of 150 the answer is 30.

Are the answers exact?

The Fraction Simplifier and the Algebra Equation Solver give exact fractions where possible and also show a decimal rounded to four places. Other results are shown to up to four decimal places.

Are my numbers saved or sent anywhere?

No. Calculations happen in your browser. Only your theme choice and, if you use the GPA calculator, your grading scale are stored on your own device.

GPA rules differ between universities. For anything official, confirm your result with your institution’s own grading policy.
