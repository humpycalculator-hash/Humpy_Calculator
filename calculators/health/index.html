About the Health calculators

These calculators give general estimates for body mass index, daily energy needs, ideal weight, body fat and water intake. They are designed to help you understand the numbers and the formulas behind them, not to diagnose or treat anything.

Estimates are based on population averages. Your own body, age, muscle mass, health history and medicines can make the real figures different. Please talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian before you change your diet, exercise or medication.

Worked examples

BMI

Weight: 70 kg

Height: 175 cm

BMI = 70 ÷ 1.75²

Answer: 22.86

BMR (Mifflin–St Jeor)

Man, 25 years, 70 kg, 175 cm

10 × 70 + 6.25 × 175 − 5 × 25 + 5

Answer: about 1,674 kcal/day

Daily calorie needs

Same person, light activity (× 1.375)

1,674 × 1.375

Answer: about 2,301 kcal/day

Tips for accurate results

  • You can switch between kilograms and pounds, and between centimetres, inches, feet and metres, so use whichever you know.
  • For Calorie Needs, choose the activity level that honestly matches your week. Picking a higher level than real life makes the estimate too high.
  • Treat every result as a starting point to discuss with a professional, not as a target or a rule.

Frequently asked questions

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI uses only height and weight, so it cannot tell muscle from fat and it ignores body shape. It is a screening number, not a diagnosis. The BMR and Calorie Needs calculators are meant for adults (18 and above).

What are BMR and calorie needs?

BMR is the energy your body uses at complete rest. Calorie Needs multiplies BMR by an activity factor to estimate the energy you use in a typical day (called TDEE). Both are estimates, not a personal diet plan.

How is body fat estimated?

The Body Fat calculator uses the US Navy circumference method, which works from waist, neck and height (and hip for women). It can be off by several percentage points, so use it to track general changes, not as a precise measurement.

How is water intake estimated?

The calculator uses about 0.033 litres per kilogram of body weight plus 0.35 litres for every 30 minutes of activity. Real needs also depend on heat, health and medicines, so follow your doctor’s advice if you have a medical condition.

Can I use these results to plan a diet?

They give a starting estimate only. For a personal plan, especially if you have a health condition, are pregnant, or are under 18, speak to a doctor or a registered dietitian.
